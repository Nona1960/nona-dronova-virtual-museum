Garden as Art

The Garden as Art

The garden is a living work in which the composition changes with light, time and season.

In The Garden as Art, natural space is regarded as a source of aesthetic experience and artistic observation. The colour combinations of plants, the rhythms of flowering, the relations of light and shadow, the alternation of textures and spatial planes form material for reflection on composition. The garden can be at once an object of contemplation, a source of painterly motifs and a space for educational experiment. The section will bring together photographs, notes, seasonal observations and related works, as verified material becomes available.

The Ferns and Moss Garden

A naturalistic woodland garden created around century-old spruce trees, many more than a hundred years old. From the very beginning the aim was to preserve every tree and the existing woodland ecosystem: native ferns, mosses, hazel and young spruce were not replaced but carefully enriched with new plantings.

A sunny front garden of roses, lilacs, plum and cherry trees forms the first threshold, built on light. Behind the house begins a shaded woodland garden of hostas, ferns, white hydrangeas and pink rhododendrons around a stone-lined stream bordered by bergenias. It is separated from the central moss garden by handcrafted wooden gates inspired by Japanese architecture and hung with small brass bells.

Many of the ferns were rescued from sites about to be developed; cushions of moss gathered from fallen trees were patiently established until they formed living carpets. Every spring the melting snow carved its own course down the slope — rather than changing its direction, it was lined with river stones, planted with wild irises and bergenias, and crossed by a small stone bridge.

Every composition should look harmonious from every viewing angle.

Creator of the garden

Creator of the Garden — Sergey Dronov

The garden was created by Sergey Dronov — Nona Dronova's husband, an artist and jeweller and a member of the Union of Artists of Russia since 2002. The garden and the paintings form the family's shared world: he makes the garden, she paints it. “The most important principle is harmony between colour, texture and the shape of the foliage. Sometimes plants should blend together; at other times they should create contrast, much like different voices in a musical composition.”
