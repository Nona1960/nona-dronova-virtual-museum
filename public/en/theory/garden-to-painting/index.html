IV. Garden, Painting and Space

From Garden to Painting

Painting from life and the mood of the season

Painting from life as conveying the mood of the season, air and light rather than an inventory of plants. The principles of the Ferns and Moss garden are set beside those of the paintings.

Nona Dronova · Essay

Original source: the artist's digital archive Mood-Paintings-Spring-by-Artist-Nona-Dronova, garden-as-art

For an artist who paints directly from nature, it is not enough to depict what is seen. The essential task is to learn how to convey the mood of the season, the quality of the air, the changing light and the emotional rhythm of nature.

A spring garden cannot be reduced to an inventory of plants. Its beauty lies in the relationships between them — between sunlight and shadow, warm and cool colours, dense foliage and transparent air, movement and stillness. Painting from nature therefore becomes an exercise in seeing beyond the object and discovering the atmosphere that unites the entire scene.

The principle of the Ferns and Moss garden is formulated by its creator, Sergey Dronov, in terms very close to the principles of the paintings: what matters most is the harmony of colour, texture and the shape of foliage; sometimes plants should blend, at other times they should contrast, ‘much like different voices in a musical composition’.

The painting preserves another kind of reality — the memory of the season as it was felt.

Questions for looking

  1. What in this painting conveys not the plant but the air and light?
  2. Compare a photograph of the garden with a painting: what did the artist keep, and what did she transform?
