Botanical Expressionism

Nona Dronova's authorial artistic concept

Museum text Text prepared by the museum · Source: Floral-Energy-Nona-Dronova

Botanical painting has traditionally been concerned with depicting plants. Nona Dronova's practice proposes a different emphasis: not only the recognisability of the plant motif, but its emotional, chromatic and plastic interpretation. The term ‘botanical expressionism’ is used to designate this research approach.

Botanical expressionism is a proposed authorial term for an artistic practice in which plant forms become the basis of an expressive statement in colour, rhythm and composition.

Six principles

  1. Principle 1

    Observation

    The initial impulse may be the close study of a living plant: its structure, its changes under light and its position in space.

  2. Principle 2

    Transformation

    The painter's task is not necessarily the exact reproduction of what is seen: form may be rethought through scale, movement, colour and compositional generalisation.

  3. Principle 3

    Colour as experience

    Colour is treated not only as a property of the depicted object but as a means of building an emotional image.

  4. Principle 4

    Rhythm and plasticity

    Repetitions, curves, directions and proportions of plant elements can form an independent compositional structure.

  5. Principle 5

    Material expressiveness

    Texture and technique take part in forming the meaning of the work rather than serving merely to convey outward likeness.

  6. Principle 6

    Interdisciplinarity

    Similar artistic questions may be explored in painting, textiles, garden composition and the plastic form of jewellery; the degree of their real connection is established by comparative analysis.

In the artist's words

A flower can become monumental. A group of flowers can become a rhythmic field. Leaves can function as directional gestures. Colour can become spatial architecture. Repetition can transform individual forms into visual movement.

For Nona Dronova painting is not only a means of making images but a method of research: each new botanical composition becomes an experiment in which the relationship between nature, perception, colour and emotional expression is tested.

Research questions

Questions for looking

  1. How is the botanical motif transformed?
  2. Which expressive means recur across different series?
  3. How do observation, emotion and artistic form relate to one another?
  4. How is the artist's method passed on through teaching?
The term ‘botanical expressionism’ is presented here as an authorial research framework. Its art-historical position, originality and limits of application require comparison with existing artistic movements and publications — see the Context section. The theory's purpose is not to replace living art with a term but to offer a language for understanding it more deeply.
