III. Botanical Form and the Artistic Image

The Leaf Must Remain a Leaf

The balance of recognition and freedom

The balance of recognition and transformation in painting the leaf: the form remains recognisable while colour is freed from the optical fact of green.

Nona Dronova · Artist's essay

Original source: the artist's digital archive Leaves-in-Painting

“When painting leaves as objects of Post-Symbolism, I am looking for a precise balance. The leaf must remain recognisable as a leaf.”

Its form, rhythm, structure and movement must still be perceived. But if it is reproduced too literally, the painting becomes merely botanical and loses its artistic tension. The challenge is to preserve recognition without becoming predictable. This is where colour becomes essential.

A leaf may appear green in nature, but painting does not have to repeat the optical fact of green. Within a single leaf one can find blue, violet, turquoise, ochre, yellow, grey or even unexpected warm tones, side by side in bold, contrasting brushstrokes. Here the painter needs courage.

The leaf must remain a leaf — but it must never become boring. The balance between recognition and transformation is where the painting begins.

Questions for looking

  1. How many different colours can you count in a single painted leaf?
  2. At what point would the leaf stop being recognisable? Where is that boundary in the work?
