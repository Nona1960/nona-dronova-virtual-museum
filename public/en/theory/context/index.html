I. The Theory of Botanical Expressionism

Historical Context

What the term ‘botanical expressionism’ should be compared with

The traditions with which botanical expressionism should be compared: botanical illustration, flower painting and the artist's garden, Fauvism and Expressionism. No claims of influence or priority are made.

Nona Dronova Virtual Art Museum · Survey article

This text was written by the museum. It claims no influences and asserts no priority: it only names the traditions with which the term ‘botanical expressionism’ needs to be compared in further art-historical analysis.

Botanical illustration

Botanical illustration serves knowledge: its task is accuracy that allows a species to be identified. Classic examples of the tradition are the works of Maria Sibylla Merian (17th–18th c.) and Pierre-Joseph Redouté (late 18th–early 19th c.). The approach described in Nona Dronova's texts is fundamentally different: recognisability is kept, but accuracy ceases to be the goal.

Flower painting and the artist's garden

European flower painting runs from the Dutch still life of the 17th century to the Impressionist garden: Claude Monet painted his own garden at Giverny for decades. The theme of ‘the artist and their garden’ is a natural point of comparison for The Garden as Art.

Expressionism and Fauvism

In the early 20th century the Fauves (Henri Matisse and others; the Salon d'Automne of 1905) and the German Expressionists (Die Brücke, from 1905, and Der Blaue Reiter, from 1911) freed colour from the obligation to describe the object. Emil Nolde painted flowers and his own garden extensively. This is the principal context in which the word ‘expressionism’ in the authorial term should be considered.

Questions for comparative analysis

Questions for looking

  1. How does the artist's ‘hierarchy of colour’ differ from the Fauvist understanding of pure colour?
  2. How does the ‘living surface’ of the impasto stroke relate to the Expressionist gesture?
  3. What does it add to the tradition of ‘the artist's garden’ that the garden and the paintings are created by different people — Sergey and Nona Dronov?
  4. How robust is the term: does it apply to textiles and jewellery, or only to painting?
