III. Botanical Form and the Artistic Image

The Energy of a Single Flower

Lilies and daylilies

Lilies and daylilies as carriers of energy and emotional states. The brushstroke as an extension of the flower's gesture; decisions found intuitively and understood later.

Nona Dronova · Artist's essay

Original source: the artist's digital archive Lilies-and-Daylilies

“My lilies are therefore not simply ‘portraits of flowers’, but studies of the emotional nature of the flower.”

“I am interested in the moment when botanical form ceases to be merely an image of nature and becomes an emotional sign. An open corolla can be perceived as a gesture. A curved petal can suggest the movement of a body. A sharp contour can resemble a flash of aggression. The deep interior of the flower creates a sense of mystery.”

The brushstroke as movement

The brushstroke should not conceal the process by which the image comes into being. Its direction, speed, density and rhythm become part of the composition. The paint moves with the form of the petal, intensifying its curve, tension or opening. The painterly gesture becomes an extension of the flower's own gesture.

Intuition before theory

“Many of its decisions emerged not from a consciously formulated theory, but intuitively… Only later, when examining the completed works, did I recognise the underlying unity of these decisions: an attempt to convey the state of a living form through colour, composition and the movement of paint.”

When the flower occupies a substantial part of the pictorial space, the viewer seems to enter its form.

Questions for looking

  1. What state does the flower convey in this work — and by what means?
  2. How does perception change when the flower is brought close to the viewer and extends beyond the edge of the canvas?
