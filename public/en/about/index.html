Artistic Identity and Creative Vision

Nona Dronova is a multidisciplinary artist whose creative practice encompasses painting, botanical art, artistic interior textiles, decorative and applied arts, and contemporary silver jewelry.

At the heart of her artistic vision lies a profound engagement with nature as a source of visual inspiration, emotional expression, and creative exploration. Flowers, gardens, botanical structures, and the changing rhythms of the natural world provide a foundation for her artistic investigations.

Her work invites viewers to consider nature not simply as a subject to be represented, but as a dynamic system of color, movement, form, and emotional relationships.

An important dimension of Dronova's artistic project is Botanical Expressionism, a developing authorial concept that explores the expressive transformation of botanical forms through painting.

Across different artistic disciplines, her practice raises a central question: how can the visual and emotional experience of nature be transformed into a distinctive artistic language?

Artistic Development: From Early Works to Botanical Collections

The development of Nona Dronova's artistic practice can be explored through her early paintings and thematic collections.

Her digital archive includes Early Works, Spring, Hydrangeas, and Peonies, offering a framework for examining the evolution of her artistic interests and visual language.

The early works provide an important starting point for investigating the formation of her artistic identity, including her approach to composition, color, materials, and expressive form.

The botanical collections introduce more focused opportunities for artistic investigation.

Spring explores a subject associated with renewal, seasonal transformation, and the changing visual atmosphere of nature.

Hydrangeas offers a rich botanical motif through which relationships between clustered forms, color transitions, spatial depth, and compositional rhythm may be examined.

Peonies provides opportunities to investigate the expressive potential of layered petals, organic volume, color intensity, and movement.

Together, these collections form an important foundation for the study of Dronova's botanical painting.

A detailed art-historical assessment of their stylistic development will be based on the examination and documentation of individual works.

Botanical Expressionism: An Authorial Artistic Concept

Botanical Expressionism occupies a central position in the proposed theoretical framework of Nona Dronova's artistic practice.

Within this framework, botanical subjects are approached not merely as objects of visual reproduction, but as starting points for expressive artistic interpretation.

The artist's response to nature becomes an active component of the creative process.

Color may move beyond descriptive accuracy to acquire emotional and compositional significance. Botanical forms may be transformed through rhythm, contrast, scale, texture, and the organization of pictorial space.

This approach invites a dialogue between observation and imagination, natural structure and artistic freedom.

The proposed theory of Botanical Expressionism is organized around six principles:

  1. Principle 1

    Emotional Interpretation of Nature

    The botanical image is understood as the result of an interaction between direct observation and the artist's individual emotional response.

  2. Principle 2

    Expressive Autonomy of Color

    Color is not limited to describing the visible characteristics of plants. It becomes an independent element of artistic expression and compositional organization.

  3. Principle 3

    Plastic Transformation of Form

    Lines, shapes, silhouettes, and textures may be transformed to communicate movement, tension, and emotional intensity.

  4. Principle 4

    Rhythmic Composition

    Repetition, contrast, variation, and the relationships between botanical elements contribute to the dynamic structure of the painting.

  5. Principle 5

    Artistic Freedom

    The botanical subject provides a point of departure rather than a fixed representational requirement. The artist may reinterpret natural forms according to the expressive needs of the composition.

  6. Principle 6

    Interdisciplinary Continuity

    Principles of color, rhythm, form, and texture may extend beyond painting into textiles, jewelry, and spatial artistic practices.

Botanical Expressionism is presented here as a developing authorial framework. Its position in relation to established art-historical movements requires further comparative research.

The Color Language of Nona Dronova

Color is a central subject for the art-historical study of Nona Dronova's painting.

In botanical art, color may function simultaneously as a descriptive element, an emotional stimulus, and a structural component of the composition.

The investigation of Dronova's individual color language focuses on the relationships between chromatic intensity, tonal contrast, warm and cool colors, and the spatial organization of color fields.

Particular attention is given to the ways in which color can transform botanical observation into an expressive artistic experience.

The concept of expressive color autonomy is especially relevant to this research.

It describes the possibility of allowing color to develop its own emotional and compositional logic rather than remaining dependent on the literal appearance of the subject.

The interaction between color and painterly texture is another important area of investigation.

Brushwork, surface structure, and the physical application of paint may contribute to the expressive identity of a botanical image.

Through the systematic examination of her paintings, the museum aims to identify recurring color relationships and compositional strategies that distinguish Dronova's artistic language.

Garden as Art: Nature as a Living Artistic Laboratory

The project Garden as Art extends the relationship between artistic practice and the natural world.

A garden can be understood as a living composition shaped by color, light, rhythm, spatial relationships, and seasonal transformation.

Unlike a completed painting, a garden exists in a continuous state of change.

Its visual structure develops through the growth of plants, variations in illumination, and the passage of time.

Within Dronova's artistic project, the garden provides a framework for exploring the relationship between natural processes and artistic perception.

It may serve as a source of botanical observation, painting, photography, theoretical reflection, and creative education.

Garden as Art introduces an important idea: the garden can be approached not only as a subject of artistic representation, but also as an evolving aesthetic environment.

Artistic Interior Textiles: Art Beyond the Canvas

Another significant dimension of Nona Dronova's creative practice is Artistic Interior Textiles.

This field connects handmade artistic production, decorative composition, material experimentation, and the aesthetics of interior space.

Textile art offers expressive possibilities distinct from those of painting.

Fabric, thread, texture, relief, color, and construction participate directly in the creation of artistic form.

An interior textile artwork may function both as an independent artistic object and as an element of the surrounding environment.

Its visual character interacts with architecture, furniture, light, and the spatial relationships of a room.

Within the museum's research program, Dronova's textile practice provides an opportunity to examine how painterly thinking may be translated into material and tactile forms.

This relationship between painting and textiles contributes to the interdisciplinary character of her artistic project.

LIBERI: An Authorial Silver Jewelry Collection

LIBERI represents Nona Dronova's artistic engagement with silver jewelry.

Jewelry occupies a distinctive position between sculpture, decorative and applied art, design, and personal adornment.

Silver offers expressive possibilities through its reflective surface, material qualities, plasticity, and interaction with light.

The artistic study of LIBERI focuses on form, construction, material, craftsmanship, and the relationship between the jewelry object and the human body.

Within the Virtual Art Museum, LIBERI is presented as an independent collection deserving detailed documentation and interpretation.

Its digital catalogue is intended to bring together images, materials, dimensions, techniques, dates, and the artist's own explanations of individual works.

The collection contributes another dimension to the broader investigation of artistic form across different media.

Theoretical Research and Artistic Inquiry

The theoretical dimension of Nona Dronova's artistic project is closely connected to the study of botanical imagery, expressive color, artistic perception, and the relationship between nature and creative practice.

The Virtual Art Museum provides a framework for collecting, organizing, and presenting her theoretical writings and research materials.

A central research objective is the systematic development of Botanical Expressionism as an authorial artistic concept.

This involves defining its principles, examining individual works, comparing relevant artistic traditions, and investigating the role of color, composition, and emotional interpretation.

Future research themes may include:

  • Botanical Expressionism: Nature, Emotion, and Artistic Form
  • The Expressive Role of Color in Contemporary Botanical Painting
  • The Garden as an Artistic Composition
  • From Painting to Textile: The Transformation of Artistic Images
  • Material and Form in Contemporary Authorial Jewelry
These are proposed research titles rather than a bibliography of verified publications. Dronova's existing theoretical works will be documented separately with their original titles, dates, and publication details.

Art Education and Creative Innovation

Art education forms an important part of the conceptual structure of Nona Dronova's Virtual Art Museum.

The proposed educational framework approaches artistic learning as a process of developing visual perception, creative independence, and individual expression.

Botanical observation offers opportunities to explore composition, color relationships, rhythm, spatial thinking, and the transformation of natural forms.

An interdisciplinary approach can extend a single artistic subject across painting, textiles, decorative composition, and three-dimensional design.

The innovative potential of this educational model lies in connecting direct observation, emotional interpretation, material experimentation, and critical reflection.

The museum aims to document Dronova's actual teaching experience, educational materials, theoretical contributions, and creative methodologies as part of its developing research archive.

Interdisciplinary Practice and Contemporary Art

Nona Dronova's artistic activities provide a foundation for examining relationships between painting, nature-based art, textiles, jewelry, and decorative design.

Rather than treating these fields as isolated disciplines, the museum proposes to investigate the ways in which artistic ideas may develop across different materials and forms.

A botanical motif may inspire a painting, a textile composition, or a spatial experiment.

A color relationship may move from a pictorial surface into the tactile environment of an interior.

An exploration of organic form may raise new questions about sculptural structure and material expression.

This interdisciplinary perspective connects Dronova's practice with broader contemporary discussions about the boundaries between fine art, craft, design, and artistic research.

Her work can therefore be examined within several overlapping contexts: contemporary botanical painting, expressive color-based art, material experimentation, decorative and applied arts, and creative education.

The Nona Dronova Virtual Art Museum

The Nona Dronova Virtual Art Museum has been conceived as a digital environment for preserving, organizing, researching, and presenting the artist's creative work.

Its structure brings together independently developed collections of paintings, garden-related projects, artistic interior textiles, and the LIBERI silver jewelry collection.

The museum is designed to connect these collections through a shared system of documentation, interpretation, and research.

Beyond presenting artworks, it aims to establish a digital archive of artistic processes, theoretical materials, educational resources, and exhibition projects.

The museum also provides an opportunity to investigate the development of Dronova's artistic language over time and across disciplines.

By combining artistic presentation with research and education, the project seeks to make her creative practice accessible to a broader international audience.

Artistic Legacy and Future Research

Nona Dronova's artistic project brings together several interconnected fields of inquiry: nature, color, artistic form, material, and education.

Her painting collections provide a foundation for investigating the expressive interpretation of botanical subjects.

Garden as Art expands this inquiry into the living environment.

Artistic Interior Textiles explores the transformation of visual ideas into tactile and spatial objects.

LIBERI introduces the distinctive expressive possibilities of silver and jewelry design.

Together, these directions form the basis of a multidisciplinary artistic practice and an evolving body of theoretical research.

The Nona Dronova Virtual Art Museum is intended to preserve this work, support its systematic study, and encourage dialogue about the relationship between nature and contemporary artistic expression.
